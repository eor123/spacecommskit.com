SCK-2400-M Module Datasheet

Version 0.1 (preliminary) — November 2026 — module rev A SpaceCommsKit — https://spacecommskit.com

Preliminary. This datasheet is written from the rev A layout. Rev A boards have not been built or tested yet. Pin assignments are final for rev A; electrical figures marked TBD will be filled in after testing.

Overview

The SCK-2400-M is the SCK-2400 radio as a solder-down module for your own board. It carries the same CC2652P, the same +20 dBm RF path and shield layout, the same external flash for the BIM bootloader and the same 10-pin programming header as the SCK-2400 board. It runs the same firmware, CCSDS protocol, AES-128-CCM encryption and over-air updates, and works with the same SCK Ground Station and SCK-PBL-1 payload board.

What it leaves out is everything a host board can provide: there is no regulator, no headers for power or serial, no SMA and no mounting holes. The module runs from 3.3 V, the antenna connects by coax to a U.FL socket, and every signal comes out on 34 castellated pins on a 2.54 mm grid.

The module has 11 GND pins. Connect all of them to the host's ground plane; they are also the RF return path.

J2 Programming Header

J2 is the same 10-pin, 1.27 mm TI debug header as on the SCK-2400 board, with the same pinout, and the same XDS110 cable fits it.

J2 pin 6 is UART0 RX, the same line as edge pin 32. Don't leave a debug probe on J2 while the Pico payload board is connected to UART0.

Power

  • 3.3 V on pins 2 and 33. Connect both. There is no regulator on the module, only the CC2652P's input decoupling and ferrite.
  • Supply range and peak current: TBD. They will be measured on rev A. Until then, size the host's 3.3 V rail for the +20 dBm transmit peaks with plenty of headroom, and keep its ripple low: the PA output depends on the supply.
  • All I/O is 3.3 V logic. None of the pins are 5 V tolerant.
  • J2 pin 1 is the module's own 3.3 V. A probe must sense it, not drive it.

Firmware

The module runs the same firmware image as the SCK-2400 board. Every DIO that the firmware uses (UART0 and UART1, the SPI0 flash, SPI1, I²C, the status LED, the bootloader backdoor and the RF switch pins) is on the same DIO on both boards, so builds, OAD updates and golden images are interchangeable.

The one hardware difference that firmware or test can notice: the I²C pull-ups R5/R6 are not fitted on the module (footprints only, fitted on request), so I²C needs pull-ups on the host board. The SCK-2400 board keeps them fitted.

Programming and Recovery

  • XDS110 on J2 with the standard 10-pin cable, or wired from the host board to pins 4 (TMSC), 5 (TCKC), 3 (RESET_N) and GND. Use cJTAG (2-pin) in CCS.
  • ROM serial bootloader: hold pin 14 (BOOT) low through a reset, then program over UART1 on pins 8 (RX) and 9 (TX). This is the same wired recovery as J4 on the SCK-2400 board; see the User Guide, Wired Recovery.
  • Over the air: OAD, trial boot and golden-image fallback work exactly as on the SCK-2400 board.

Never hold BOOT (pin 14) low in flight. With it low at reset, the module always starts the ROM bootloader.

Mount the module either way:

  • Flat: solder the castellations directly to pads on the host. Lowest profile, best for a CubeSat stack.
  • Raised: fit 2.54 mm pin headers through the plated holes and plug or solder them into the host, with spacers, so the host can have parts underneath.

Designing the Host Board

  • Footprint: two rows of 17 pads at 2.54 mm pitch, rows 30.48 mm apart, pin 1 top-left. For flat mounting, extend each pad about 1 mm outside the module outline so the castellation fillet can be inspected.
  • Ground: connect all 11 GND pins straight to a solid ground plane.
  • Under the module: keep exposed copper and vias out from under the module when it's soldered flat, especially under the RF section at the east end.
  • Power: a 3.3 V rail with bulk and 100 nF decoupling next to pins 2 and 33.
  • I²C: pull-ups are required on the host if you use pins 10/11. Fit 4.7 kΩ to 3.3 V on SCL and SDA (or your bus's own value). The module leaves them off so it never adds a second set to an existing bus.
  • Recovery access: bring out BOOT (14), RESET_N (3), UART1 (8, 9) and GND to a test point or header on the host, so a module can be reflashed after integration.
  • Antenna: route coax from the U.FL to the antenna; keep the cable away from the module's digital side.

Status and Open Items

  • To be measured on rev A: supply range, transmit and idle current, output power through the U.FL, height and thermal behaviour when raised on headers.
